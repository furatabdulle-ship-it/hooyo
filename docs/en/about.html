About Hawo

Hawo
Abdulle.

Building Communities. Empowering Women. Making Voices Heard.

Hawo Abdulle was born in 1968 in Qalafe, Somalia. She came to Germany in 1989—a country that became her home. Today she is married and the mother of nine children, all born and raised in Germany. For nearly four decades, she has brought together family, civic engagement and work with people.

She has helped build associations, created spaces for people to meet, counselled women and contributed to an internationally acclaimed film. Her work takes place where people encounter social, language or institutional barriers—and where personal experience, trust and practical support can make a difference.

Today, she brings this experience to collaborations with women, social organisations, educational institutions and projects that aim to reach and support people and contribute to social change.

Community begins when someone takes the first step

In 2005, Hawo helped establish an association in Darmstadt whose initial work included education, integration and counselling for the Somali community, as well as humanitarian projects connected to Somalia.

In 2008/2009, she was among the founders of the International Women’s Café Groß-Gerau and Surrounding Area e. V. She chaired the association from 2009 to 2021. For more than twelve years, she helped create a space where women from different backgrounds could meet, exchange ideas and support one another.

She organised shared breakfasts, events and activities. Conversations addressed the everyday concerns of women and families: education, health, children, public services and life in Germany.

In 2016, Hawo received the German Citizens’ Award in the “Everyday Heroes” category in the district of Groß-Gerau. She continued her work during the COVID-19 pandemic, organising an exercise programme at the Anne Frank Youth Centre.

What she brings today

Hawo knows from experience what it takes to build and sustain community programmes over time. She understands the importance of trust, lasting relationships and accessible ways to take part.

This experience is especially relevant to organisations seeking to reach women and migrant communities, develop spaces for people to meet, or make participation possible in practice—not just in planning.

Women’s rights begin when support is accessible

Hawo now works at FIM – Frauenrecht ist Menschenrecht e. V. in Frankfurt am Main. She focuses on counselling women affected by female genital mutilation/cutting (FGM/C), particularly in relation to Somalia.

Her work brings together personal experiences, health questions, social taboos, language barriers and institutional systems. Hawo helps women understand information, learn about counselling and care services, and find their way through complex situations.

What she brings today

Her experience is valuable to projects that communicate sensitive health topics clearly, take language and cultural barriers into account, or actively involve affected women in developing support services.

She connects community experience with practical counselling and understands that good services can only work when they reach the people they are intended for.

HALIMA – Women telling their own stories

Some experiences remain largely invisible in public life. Through HALIMA – Eine Anleitung zum Starksein, Hawo contributed to a film project that centres the perspectives of women who have experienced FGM/C.

The film follows a woman after reconstructive surgery. Her journey home becomes a story about war, displacement, migration, bodily integrity and self-determination. The main character draws on the experiences of several women, and the film was made collaboratively.

Hawo contributed to the screenplay, supported the production as an interpreter on set and was part of the film collective. HALIMA screened at international film festivals and received several awards, including recognition from the German Society of Plastic, Reconstructive and Aesthetic Surgeons and at the SHORTS Film Festival Offenburg.

What she brings today

The project demonstrates her experience in working with people affected by sensitive social issues to bring their perspectives into public storytelling.

For film, education and awareness projects, she can contribute community perspectives, language mediation and experience from participatory film production.

Experience connecting people and organisations

Hawo Abdulle’s life brings together many roles: mother, association founder, long-serving chair, women’s rights counsellor and contributor to an international film project.

What defines her work is not one award or position, but the continuity of her commitment. For decades, she has worked with people, built relationships, organised opportunities to meet and engaged with questions that rarely have simple answers.

That experience now informs the different ways she can contribute to collaboration.
